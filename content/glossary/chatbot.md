A chatbot is any computer program built for conversation. Older chatbots followed fixed scripts: they recognised certain keywords and picked a prepared answer. Newer ones, such as the AI assistants many people now use, are powered by a [large language model](/glossary/large-language-model) and can respond to almost anything you type.

You may meet one on a shop's website when you ask where your parcel is, or use one at home to help plan a week of meals. You type a question, which is called a [prompt](/glossary/prompt), and it writes back.

A chatbot can sound friendly and sure of itself, but it is not a person and does not understand you the way a friend would. It can get facts wrong. Avoid sharing private details such as passwords or health records unless you know how the service stores your conversations.
