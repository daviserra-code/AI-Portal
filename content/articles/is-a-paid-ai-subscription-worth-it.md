Most people don't need to pay for an AI [chatbot](/glossary/chatbot). The free versions of ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude and Microsoft Copilot answer questions, draft emails and explain things well enough for occasional use. What a subscription buys is mostly more: more messages before you hit a limit, access to the makers' stronger [models](/glossary/model), more pictures, and on some plans storage or office apps thrown in.

This guide looks at the money question only. If you are still deciding which assistant suits you, start with our comparison of [which AI assistant to use](/use/chatbots-compared). All prices below are as shown on the companies' own pricing pages in October 2026, in US dollars, unless we say otherwise.

## What do the free versions give you now?

More than you might expect, with caps on the expensive features.

- **ChatGPT Free** offers "unlimited" text chats, "subject to abuse guardrails", according to OpenAI's pricing page. Uploads, image creation, voice chats, deep research and memory are all available but limited.
- **Gemini** (Google) lists image generation and editing, Deep Research, the Gemini Live voice mode and 15 GB of storage across Gmail, Drive and Photos on its free plan. Free users get Google's lightest model, Flash-Lite.
- **Claude** (Anthropic) on the free plan can search the web, create files and run code, with memory and up to five projects. Anthropic calls its usage "the lowest tier" and limits it to its Sonnet and Haiku models.
- **Microsoft Copilot** is free with a Microsoft account, with what Microsoft calls "limited usage".

"Limited" usually means you can work normally for a while, then get told to wait a few hours or switch to a weaker model. Anthropic, for example, resets usage on a rolling five-hour window. If that never happens to you, you are probably not the person a subscription is for.

## Do free chatbots show ads now?

ChatGPT does. OpenAI said ads would start on ChatGPT's Free and Go plans in 31 European countries on 24 August 2026, [Euronews reported](https://www.euronews.com/2026/08/21/chatgpt-to-start-showing-ads-across-europe-next-week), after launches in the US, UK and several other markets.

OpenAI's [help page on ads](https://help.openai.com/en/articles/20001047-ads-in-chatgpt) says Plus, Pro and business accounts are ad-free, that accounts identified as under 18 see no ads, and that ads are not shown next to conversations about health, mental health or politics. Advertisers "do not have access to your chats", OpenAI says. You can turn off ad personalisation, which stops ChatGPT using your other chats to pick ads, but it doesn't remove them. OpenAI also offers an ad-free free option with "fewer messages per day and no access to tools like images."

Anthropic said in February 2026 that Claude would stay ad-free, MacRumors reported, though the company left room to revisit that. The Google and Microsoft pages we checked don't mention ads in their chatbots.

## What does a roughly $20 plan add?

The main paid tier at each company costs about the same:

| Plan | Price per month | What it mainly adds, per the maker |
|---|---|---|
| ChatGPT Plus | $20 | Advanced reasoning models, more messages and uploads, better image creation, more deep research, projects and scheduled tasks, no ads |
| Claude Pro | $20, or $17 a month paid yearly ($200 up front) | At least five times Free's usage, the stronger Opus model, Claude Code, Claude in Chrome |
| Google AI Pro | $19.99 | Four times Free's limits, Google's Pro model, video generation, Gemini in Gmail and Docs, 5 TB storage, YouTube Premium Lite |
| Microsoft 365 Personal | $9.99, or $99.99 a year | Copilot inside Word, Excel, PowerPoint and Outlook, higher Copilot limits, 1 TB OneDrive storage |

There are cheaper steps too. **ChatGPT Go**, which Euronews puts at $8 a month, adds more messages with tools, more uploads and images and longer memory, but it can show ads. **Google AI Plus** at $4.99 doubles Gemini's free limits, adds video generation and 400 GB of storage.

Two of these are really bundles. Google's plans fold in Google One storage, so if you already pay for extra Google storage for your photos, the AI may cost little extra. Microsoft's Copilot comes with the Office apps and OneDrive. If you would pay for storage or Office anyway, compare the bundle against what you pay now rather than against a chatbot alone.

## Who are the $100 to $200 plans for?

People who use AI for hours every working day and keep hitting the $20 limits, usually for programming, long research or heavy image work.

- **ChatGPT Pro** comes in two tiers named Pro $100 and Pro $200, with five and twenty times the usage of Plus. OpenAI says it is "designed for people who rely on AI to get high-stakes, complex work done". New sign-ups to the $200 tier were paused on 10 September 2026, according to OpenAI's help page.
- **Claude Max** starts at $100 a month, with five or twenty times Pro's usage.
- **Google AI Ultra** costs $99.99 or $199.99 a month, with five or twenty times AI Pro's limits and at least 20 TB of storage.

Microsoft takes a different line: its top personal plan, **Microsoft 365 Premium**, costs $19.99 a month or $199.99 a year, and Microsoft describes its Copilot usage as "extensive", with an [AI agent](/glossary/ai-agent) for research and analysis.

If you are wondering whether you need one of these, you almost certainly don't.

## Are there family or student deals?

A few, and they differ a lot.

- **Students.** Google is offering eligible college students in the US a free year of AI Pro, and students elsewhere a free year of AI Plus, if they sign up by 31 December 2026, MacRumors reports. You need to be 18 or over and verify with a school email. Billing starts automatically after the year unless you cancel.
- **Families with Google.** Google says family plan members on AI Pro "can enjoy select AI benefits and features at no extra cost", though its help page does not list all of them. iThinkDiff reports that AI Plus storage can be shared with up to five other family members.
- **Families with Microsoft.** Microsoft 365 Family costs $12.99 a month for up to six people with 2 TB of storage shared, but Microsoft says its AI features are for the subscription owner only.
- **ChatGPT and Claude** have no family plan, according to iThinkDiff: each paid plan is for one person.

## Does paying protect my privacy?

Not on its own. On personal accounts, the training rules are the same whether you pay or not. OpenAI says it may use content from ChatGPT Free, Plus and Pro to train its models, and only its Business, Enterprise and Edu plans are excluded by default. Anthropic's training choice covers Claude Free, Pro and Max alike. Google's privacy hub describes the same Keep Activity setting for all Gemini users and says work or school accounts may have different terms.

So a subscription buys you no ads on ChatGPT, but not fewer eyes on your chats. You still need to switch off training yourself. Our guide to [your data and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/your-data-and-ai-chatbots) shows where each setting is.

## So should you pay? A quick guide by type of user

**Occasional user.** You ask a chatbot something a few times a week. Stay free. If ads on ChatGPT bother you, try another assistant's free version before paying to remove them.

**Student.** Check the student offers first, and check whether your university already provides an AI tool. Be wary of anything that renews into a full price after a free year: put the date in your calendar.

**Writer or office worker.** If you write all day inside Word or Gmail, the cheapest useful option may be the bundle you half pay for already: Microsoft 365 or a Google storage plan. If you use a standalone chatbot daily and hit its limits most weeks, a $20 plan is reasonable. If the work is your employer's, ask for a work account rather than paying yourself, because business plans are not used for training by default.

**Heavy user.** You run long documents, code or research through a chatbot for hours. A $20 plan will likely pay for itself in saved time. Move up to a $100 plan only after you have hit the $20 limits repeatedly, and drop back down if a month goes by when you didn't.

## How do I try a plan without getting stuck?

Use the free version for a couple of weeks and note what actually stops you: message limits, a missing feature, image quality. Then pay for one month of the plan that fixes that specific thing, not the biggest one.

Paying monthly makes cancelling easy. OpenAI says Go "can be canceled anytime", and ChatGPT subscriptions are cancelled under Settings, then Account. Yearly plans are cheaper per month, but only once you know you will use them.

Finally, check the total at checkout. The prices above are the US ones shown on the makers' pages. Anthropic says its prices "don't include applicable tax", and in the EU, VAT and local currency usually make the amount you pay higher. Plans, prices and limits change often, and every page we used says so.
