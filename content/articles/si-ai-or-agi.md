## What happened

On 29 September 2026, President Trump signed an executive order titled "Inaugurating the Era of Super Intelligence". It tells US federal agencies to replace "artificial intelligence" and "AI" with "super intelligence" and "SI" in their official communications, websites, reports and policy documents, "to the maximum extent permitted by law".

The stated reason is about ambition. In the President's words, the term "more appropriately captures the promise, potential, and rapidly advancing capabilities of these technologies."

On the same day, six major AI companies (Anthropic, Google, Meta, Nvidia, OpenAI and xAI) signed a separate, voluntary "White House Accord on Super Intelligence". It asks them to set up internal oversight, independent audits and a board committee to review the results. It carries no legal penalties.

## What the order does not change

- **The law.** Existing statutes, regulations, contracts and historical documents are not rewritten. For now, "super intelligence" points to the definition of AI already in US law (15 U.S.C. 9401(3)).
- **The technology.** The chatbot on your phone and the model behind your email's autocomplete are exactly what they were last week.
- **Everyone else.** The order applies to US federal agencies. Companies, researchers, other governments and the rest of us are free to keep saying "AI", and most do.

## Why the new term is confusing

Before September, "superintelligence" already had a specific meaning. Researchers used it for a hypothetical future AI that greatly exceeds human ability in almost every field, not just at chess or writing code. The philosopher Nick Bostrom made the idea widely known in his 2014 book *Superintelligence*.

No one claims today's systems have reached that point. So when you read "SI" now, it is worth asking which meaning the writer has in mind: the US government's new label for all AI, or the researchers' idea of something far more powerful.

| Term | What it usually means | Exists today? |

## What it means for you

- **Reading the news:** "SI" in a US government document means AI. It is not a sign of a sudden breakthrough.
- **At work:** if you deal with US federal agencies, expect "SI" in contracts and guidance that are new or updated, while older ones keep "AI". Both terms refer to the same things for now.
- **Everywhere else:** nothing changes. EU rules such as the AI Act still say "AI", and so do the tools you use.
