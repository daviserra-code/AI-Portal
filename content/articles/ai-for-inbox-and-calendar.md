The AI you need for your inbox is probably already there. Gmail, Outlook and Apple Mail all now have a button that summarises a long thread, suggests a reply or turns an email into a calendar invite. And if you use a [chatbot](/glossary/chatbot) such as ChatGPT or Claude, you can connect it to your email and calendar and ask questions about them in plain language.

These tools are good at the dull part: reading, condensing, drafting, finding a date. They are less good at knowing what matters to you, and they make mistakes that look convincing. So the useful habit is the same everywhere: let the AI do the first pass, and keep the decisions for yourself.

## What can the AI built into my email already do?

**Gmail and Google Calendar (Gemini).** Google's help page lists several Gemini features in Gmail. The most widely available is the AI Overview, a summary at the top of a long thread with its key points. For personal Gmail accounts the page lists it as "Global", in eight languages including English, French, German, Italian and Spanish. **Help me write** (drafting) and **Suggested Replies** are free on personal accounts too, but only in the US; elsewhere they need a paid Google AI plan or a work Workspace account.

Newer features are paid and mostly limited to the US. Google announced in January 2026 that subscribers to Google AI Pro and Ultra can ask their whole inbox questions, and get a **Proofread** tool. The **AI Inbox**, which sorts mail into priorities and things to catch up on, is listed as English and "US only" on Google AI Plus, Pro or Ultra and some Workspace plans.

For the calendar, Google says every Gmail user can turn an email thread into a calendar event, with the people on the thread added as guests. Having Gemini suggest meeting times inside an email needs a paid plan. The separate Gemini app can also create calendar events, add reminders to Google Tasks and save lists to Google Keep, once you connect it to your Google apps.

**Outlook (Copilot).** Microsoft's help pages say Copilot in Outlook can draft an email from a short instruction, summarise a thread with numbered links back to the messages it used, and, in the new Outlook, build a meeting invitation from a thread with the participants already added. It works with work or school accounts and with outlook.com, hotmail.com, live.com and msn.com addresses. If you read Gmail or iCloud mail inside Outlook, Copilot is not available for those accounts.

At home you need a paid plan. Microsoft says Copilot came to Outlook for Microsoft 365 Personal and Family subscribers in 2025, with usage limits. Office Watch reports the limit as 60 AI credits a month for the account owner, and that people sharing a Family plan get none. The more expensive Microsoft 365 Premium raises the limits.

**Apple Mail (Apple Intelligence).** Apple's Mail can show a short summary under each unread email instead of the first lines, summarise a whole thread, put time-sensitive messages at the top as **Priority Messages**, and offer **Smart Reply** suggestions you edit before sending. It costs nothing extra but needs a recent device: Apple lists the iPhone 15 Pro, the iPhone 16 and later, and Macs and iPads with an M1 chip or newer. Apple Intelligence reached the EU with iOS 18.4 at the end of March 2025, according to 9to5Mac.

## Can ChatGPT or Claude read my inbox?

Yes, if you connect them. This is different from the built-in tools: you give a chatbot company permission to search your mail and calendar so you can ask things like "what did the school say about the trip?" or "when am I free next week?"

Anthropic's help page says Claude's Gmail and Google Calendar connectors are available to all users. Claude can search and read email, draft messages, and send, reply and forward, asking for your approval before each of those actions by default. On the calendar it can find free time and create, change or delete events. (Anthropic makes Claude, the AI used to help draft this article.)

OpenAI offers similar connections in ChatGPT, called apps. When it launched apps in October 2025, OpenAI said they were not available in the European Economic Area, Switzerland or the UK, and that it expected to bring them to EU users "soon". Check what your own account offers. In late October 2026 OpenAI added iCloud Mail and Calendar for all plans on web, desktop and iOS, MacRumors reports, including the ability to send email and change events. Notebookcheck points out that this uses an Apple app-specific password, which stays valid after you disconnect in ChatGPT until you delete it in your Apple account.

## Is it any good at summarising long threads?

For a quick sense of a 40-message thread, often yes. The risk is that a summary sounds sure of itself even when it has dropped a condition, mixed up who said what or invented a detail. That kind of error is called a [hallucination](/glossary/hallucination).

Apple learned this in public. In January 2025 it switched off its AI summaries of news notifications after, as MobileSyrup reports, a summary of a BBC News alert falsely said that Luigi Mangione had shot himself. Apple added a warning in Settings that summaries may contain errors. Your email summaries come from the same kind of system.

Use summaries to decide what to read, not instead of reading. Microsoft's citations, which link each point back to a message, make checking easier: click through on anything you are about to act on.

## Can it write my replies?

It can write a decent first draft, and the built-in buttons make it quick. Our guide to [using AI for everyday writing](/use/ai-for-everyday-writing) covers prompts for drafting and what to check. The short version for email: check names, dates, amounts and promises, because an AI will happily add an apology or a commitment you never made. A clear [prompt](/glossary/prompt) helps: say what the reply must include and tell it not to add anything else.

## Can it sort out my calendar and to-do list?

This is where AI saves real effort, because the work is mostly copying details from one place to another. Practical uses that work today:

- **Email to event.** Gmail's "create an event from an email" and Outlook's "Schedule with Copilot" fill in the title, guests and details for you to check.
- **Finding a time.** Claude's calendar connector can look for mutual free time, and paid Gemini can suggest times inside an email.
- **Pulling out tasks.** Ask a connected chatbot: "List everything in my inbox from the last week that asks me to do something, with the deadline." Google says the Gemini app can then add items to Google Tasks.

Check the date, time and time zone of anything the AI puts in your calendar. A meeting at 3pm in the wrong time zone is the classic mistake.

## Should I let an agent handle my email for me?

An [AI agent](/glossary/ai-agent) goes further than a summary button: it takes steps on its own, such as searching your inbox, drafting replies and sending them. Several of the tools above can now send mail or change events, not just suggest. Our explainer on [AI agents](/understand/ai-agents-explained) covers which ones exist and where they fail.

For email, the sensible line today is: let the agent prepare, and do the sending yourself. Keep any "ask before sending" setting switched on. A wrong sentence in a draft costs you nothing. A wrong email sent to your boss, or a meeting cancelled for twenty people, cannot be taken back.

## What can go wrong?

**Wrong summaries and drafts.** Covered above, and the most common problem.

**Sending things you didn't check.** The faster the tool, the easier it is to press send on something you skimmed. Recipients and attachments are your job.

**Prompt injection.** An AI reading your email cannot reliably tell your instructions from text inside a message. In July 2025 researchers reported through the security firm 0DIN that hidden text in an email, such as white-on-white writing, could make Gemini's "Summarize this email" show a fake "password compromised" warning with a scam phone number, TechRepublic and Dark Reading reported. Google said it had seen no such attacks in the wild and was rolling out more defences. In June 2025 Aim Security disclosed EchoLeak, a flaw in Microsoft 365 Copilot that could leak data from a single crafted email without the user clicking anything. Microsoft said it had fixed it and no customer action was needed.

The lesson: never act on a warning, link or phone number that appears only in an AI summary. Open the original email, and contact the company through its own website or app.

**Privacy.** Connecting a chatbot gives a company access to some of the most personal data you have, including other people's messages to you. Policies differ. Anthropic says it does not train its models on data from its Google connectors, but that if you have allowed training, content you copy from them into a chat may be used. Notebookcheck notes that for ChatGPT Free, Go, Plus and Pro, content from connected apps may be used for training while "Improve the model for everyone" is on. Google's Gemini app requires its Keep Activity setting to be on before it can connect to Gmail, and Google's privacy notice says it uses Connected Apps information "to provide and improve Gemini Apps". Our guide to [your data and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/your-data-and-ai-chatbots) shows where the training switches are.

## Which habits keep it useful and safe?

- **Start read-only.** Ask questions and get summaries before you let anything send or delete.
- **Approve every send.** Keep confirmations on, and read the whole message before you agree.
- **Open the original** before acting on anything that involves money, passwords or a deadline.
- **Turn off training** in the chatbot's settings before you connect your mail.
- **Keep work and personal apart.** Don't connect your work inbox to a personal chatbot. Your employer may already have an approved tool.
- **Disconnect what you don't use,** and for iCloud also delete the app-specific password at Apple.
