This was a week about trust: whether the biggest AI companies can be trusted to police themselves, and whether the AI tools in your pocket can be trusted with your money, your logins and your conversations. Here are the seven stories that matter most.

## Campaign groups ask a court to stop the most advanced AI

On 12 November a group of nonprofits sued OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, Meta and SpaceX's AI division in San Francisco, [Reuters reports](https://thedailyrecord.com/2026/11/12/nonprofits-sue-openai-google-anthropic-halt-frontier-ai/). The plaintiffs include Public Citizen, Heat Initiative and the Irreplaceable Education Fund, and [Dataconomy](https://dataconomy.com/2026/11/13/nonprofits-sue-openai-google-and-meta-to-halt-advanced-ai) adds the Alliance for Secure AI. They want a court order stopping further development of the companies' most capable [models](/glossary/model), the systems behind ChatGPT, Gemini and Claude.

The case rests on California's law against unfair business practices and on "public nuisance", the legal idea that something harms the public as a whole. The complaint argues that "no other industry" may treat building unsafe products as inevitable, or regulate itself. It cites company leaders' own warnings about AI's dangers. Meta declined to comment, and the other companies did not respond straight away.

What it means: nothing changes for your chatbot today, and a judge would have to agree to an unusually sweeping order. The case adds to a pile of legal pressure: Reuters notes that Florida, and separately a group of eight states, are already suing OpenAI. Our explainer on [AI safety](/super-intelligence/what-is-ai-safety) covers the risks these groups are worried about.

## Chatbots sometimes send shoppers to fake stores

A study by Podqi, a firm that protects brands from counterfeiters, asked ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity where to buy products from 151 brands, including Birkenstock and Le Creuset, [Digit reports](https://www.digit.in/news/general/shopping-with-ai-chatgpt-gemini-and-other-ai-chatbots-can-lead-you-to-fake-websites-study-finds.html). Across 6,795 answers, risky or possibly fake sites came up in 15% of Perplexity's answers, 8% of ChatGPT's and 4% of Gemini's. Smaller brands were hit about twice as often as big ones, and in one case Perplexity called a lookalike site Stüssy's official shop.

All three companies told reporters they work on accuracy, and Google pointed out that Gemini links to its sources so people can check.

What it means: with holiday shopping starting, treat a [chatbot's](/glossary/chatbot) shop link like a link from a stranger. Before you type card details, check that the web address matches the brand's real site, ideally by going to it yourself. Our guide to [spotting AI-generated content](/live-with-it/spotting-ai-generated-content) has more habits that help.

## United Airlines shuts out AI booking assistants

United said on 12 November that it will block AI assistants that book flights and manage trips on a customer's behalf, both on its own website and app and through travel agencies, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/united-airlines-blocks-ai-agents-from-booking-flights-citing-cybersecurity-risks/). It warned that "sharing account credentials with personal, third-party, AI assistants can expose sensitive information" and could lead to purchases, changes or use of air miles you didn't intend. It also said such tools may break federal rules, such as the one requiring a person to complete check-in and confirm what is in their bags.

TechCrunch notes that Bank of America warned customers the week before not to give AI assistants their banking passwords.

What it means: [AI agents](/glossary/ai-agent), tools that act for you rather than just answer questions, are running into companies that don't want them. If an AI tool asks for your password to an airline, bank or shopping account, think twice: you may break the account's terms and lose protection if something goes wrong. Our explainer on [AI agents](/understand/ai-agents-explained) covers how they work.

## A breach at an AI supplier exposed some Notion users' AI chats

Hackers broke into the cloud provider used by Fireworks AI, a service that lets companies run [open-weights models](/glossary/open-weights-model), and reached customer data, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/cyberattack-on-fireworks-ai-allowed-hackers-to-access-notion-customers-chat-transcripts/). It is Fireworks' second breach in two months. Notion, the note-taking app, confirmed that chat transcripts from its AI feature were taken, including "any input to or output from the model that was written to the chat". Notion says about 1% of its customers were affected and that it cut its ties with Fireworks on 3 November.

What it means: when you type into an AI feature inside an app, your words may pass through other companies you have never heard of. Keep passwords, health details and confidential work out of AI chats where you can. Our guide to [your data and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/your-data-and-ai-chatbots) explains where your chats go.

## Gemini glasses go on preorder

Google and Samsung opened preorders on 12 November for smart glasses with Google's Gemini assistant built in, sold by Warby Parker and Gentle Monster, [The Next Web reports](https://thenextweb.com/news/google-samsung-smart-glasses-preorders-warby-parker-gentle-monster). Prices run from $395 to $489, and they ship on 10 December in the US, with Gentle Monster styles also on sale in South Korea. You can say "Hey Gemini" to ask about what you're looking at, get directions or translate a conversation.

The glasses carry a 12-megapixel camera. According to The Next Web, the camera stops working if its recording light is covered, faces of bystanders are blurred before Gemini sees an image, and buyers must be 18 and agree not to film people without permission.

What it means: AI that sees what you see is moving from phones onto faces. If you meet someone wearing these, a lit recording light means the camera is on. Our piece on [AI privacy at home](/live-with-it/ai-privacy-at-home) covers cameras and microphones in everyday devices.

## New York starts signing up the biggest AI labs

New York opened a registration portal on 10 November for its AI safety law, the RAISE Act, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/10/openai-anthropic-face-new-oversight-as-new-yorks-ai-safety-law-takes-effect/). It covers AI developers with more than $500 million in yearly revenue, such as OpenAI and Anthropic. They must register by 1 January 2027, publish their safety plans, issue reports on their models and tell the state about serious safety incidents within 72 hours. Fines reach $1 million for a first violation and $3 million for later ones.

What it means: you won't notice a difference in the apps you use. The rules mainly force companies to put their safety promises in writing where regulators can check them. Our explainer on [who regulates AI](/super-intelligence/who-regulates-ai) shows how state laws like this fit alongside national and European rules.

## Nvidia's chief executive is out

Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia and led it for 33 years, resigned as chief executive on 12 November, the board announced, [TechCrunch reports](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/12/jensen-huang-is-out-at-nvidia-who-is-colette-kress-his-interim-replacement/). Finance chief Colette Kress is interim boss. [TechCrunch reported on 14 November](https://techcrunch.com/2026/11/14/elon-musk-other-tech-execs-sign-petition-calling-on-nvidia-to-reinstate-jensen-huang/) that he left days after acknowledging a relationship with a chip executive whom US intelligence has identified as a suspected Chinese spy. A petition asking the board to reinstate him while an independent investigation runs had more than 115,000 signatures by Saturday, including Elon Musk's.

What it means: Nvidia makes most of the chips that train and run today's AI, and TechCrunch puts its value at about $3.8 trillion, so it sits in many pension and index funds. Nothing changes for the AI tools you use. Our explainer on [AI chips](/understand/ai-chips-and-gpus) explains why one company matters so much.
