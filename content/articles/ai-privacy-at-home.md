## Is my smart speaker always listening?

It is always listening for one thing: its wake word. Amazon says an Echo matches the "acoustic patterns" of the wake word on the device itself. Google says that in standby a speaker running Gemini for Home "won't send what you are saying to Google or anyone else." Only after the device hears its wake word (or something that sounds like it) does it record your request and send it to the company's servers.

That second step matters more than it used to. The new assistants are built on [generative AI](/glossary/generative-ai): large [models](/glossary/model) that run in data centres, not on a small speaker. So almost every request now goes to the cloud, and what happens there depends on your settings.

A light tells you when this is happening. On an Echo, the ring turns blue. Google says the indicator on its devices "will remain visible during mic activity." The physical mute button is the only way to be sure nothing is sent: Amazon says pressing it cuts power to the microphones.

## Amazon Alexa and Alexa+

**What changed.** In March 2025 Amazon ended an option called "Do Not Send Voice Recordings", which let a few Echo models process requests on the device. Its email to customers said that "as we continue to expand Alexa's capabilities with generative AI features" it had "decided to no longer support this feature," TechCrunch reported. According to AppleInsider, the option only ever existed in the US, so most European owners never had it. Since 28 March 2025, Echo requests go to Amazon's cloud.

Alexa+, the new AI version, then reached Europe in 2026. Amazon's launch page lists early access in the UK from 19 March, Italy from 15 April, Spain from 23 April, Germany and Austria from 7 May and France from 26 May. After early access it costs €22.99 a month in those EU countries for people without Prime.

**What Amazon keeps.** Amazon's UK help pages say each request is recorded, uploaded and turned into a text transcript. In the Alexa app, under **More**, then **Alexa Privacy**, you can choose to auto-delete recordings and transcripts after 3 or 18 months, or **Don't save recordings**. With "Don't save", Amazon says recordings are deleted after processing, but transcripts and typed requests stay for 30 days after your last interaction in a chat. It also deletes the Voice IDs on the account, so Alexa may stop recognising who is speaking.

Amazon says humans review "an extremely small sample" of interactions and that your interactions help train its machine learning models. It adds a caveat worth knowing: "we may continue to retain and use the systems trained on your data after your data has been deleted." The same is true of chatbots, as our guide to [your data and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/your-data-and-ai-chatbots) explains.

## Google Gemini for Home

Gemini for Home replaces Google Assistant on Nest speakers and displays. 9to5Google reports that early access reached 16 more countries on 8 April 2026, including France, Italy, Spain, the Netherlands, Belgium, Ireland, Austria and the Nordic countries. You have to opt in through the Google Home app.

Google's help page describes two separate switches:

- **Home History** saves what you ask, when Voice Match is set up. While it is on, Google says it uses your activity to improve its services, "including training generative AI models." By default it auto-deletes after 18 months; you can pick 3 or 36 months instead.
- **Improve Google services with your voice, audio, and Gemini Live recordings** lets Google keep and use the audio itself. Google says this one is off by default.

Find both in the Google Home app under **Profile**, **Home settings**, **Privacy**, **Home History**, or delete past activity at myactivity.google.com/product/home.

Visitors get some protection. Google says that for guests it does not recognise, it does not use their audio or transcripts "to train our AI models," though it keeps transcripts for a while for troubleshooting.

## Apple Siri and HomePod

Apple's position, in a January 2025 statement, is that it "does not retain audio recordings of Siri interactions unless users explicitly opt in" through the **Improve Siri & Dictation** setting, and that Siri requests are not tied to your Apple Account. Bigger requests go to what Apple calls Private Cloud Compute, where it says your data "is not stored or made accessible to Apple."

Apple's rebuilt assistant, Siri AI, uses models that Apple says were "custom-built in collaboration with Google and its Gemini models." When it launched in September 2026, Apple said it would not initially be available in the EU on iPhone and iPad. The new HomePod mini announced in October 2026 gets Siri AI later through a software update, and the countries Apple lists for that do not include any EU member. For now, most European HomePod owners have the older Siri. These privacy claims are Apple's own; we could not test them.

## What do AI doorbells and cameras do with video?

The AI features on cameras are about understanding footage, and that work happens in the cloud.

**Google Nest cameras.** For subscribers to Google Home Premium Advanced, Gemini writes descriptions of what a camera saw, sends a daily summary called Home Brief and lets you search footage in plain words. Google's list of countries for AI descriptions includes Germany, France, Italy, Spain and most of the EU. Its privacy hub says Nest video is kept "for the duration of your recording subscription period," is not used for ad personalisation, and is not used to improve models or shown to human reviewers unless you separately consent.

**Ring and face recognition.** Ring's Familiar Faces feature recognises people you have named, such as "Mom at Front Door", and sends an alert with their name. It launched in the US on 9 December 2025 and reached the UK in April 2026. Ring's UK support page now says it is available in the UK, France, Spain, Italy, the Netherlands and Germany. It is off by default and needs a paid plan.

Ring's own page sets out what that means:

- Face data is encrypted and stored in Ring's cloud, not on your doorbell.
- Faces you haven't named are deleted after 30 days.
- It does not work with Ring's end-to-end video encryption, so you have to choose one or the other.
- Ring recommends telling visitors their facial data is processed, notes that consent from children or other vulnerable people comes with particular conditions, and says that if someone withdraws consent you must delete their profile without delay.

The feature is contested. In June 2026 a Virginia man sued Amazon and Ring, claiming it creates facial data from visitors and passers-by who never agreed, The Register reported; Ring says the feature is opt-in. Separately, in February 2026 Ring cancelled a planned partnership with the surveillance company Flock, amid criticism of a Super Bowl advert for its AI pet-finding feature; Ring said the work needed more time and resources than expected. Both are US stories, but they show where the arguments lie.

## What does EU law say about filming visitors and neighbours?

Under the GDPR, a camera that films only your own property is usually your private business. That changes once it captures anyone else's space. Ireland's Data Protection Commission, in guidance updated in May 2026, says the household exemption does not apply if images are captured outside your property, citing the EU Court of Justice's Ryneš ruling. It says "it is not acceptable" to film your neighbours' homes, gardens or driveways, and that the same rules apply to smart doorbells as to fixed cameras. Its advice: use a privacy zone or change the camera's angle, and don't post footage online.

The Dutch data protection authority is seeing the problem grow. In September 2025 it said it had received 370 complaints and 702 tips about private doorbell and security cameras in 2024, 20% more than in 2023, and that features like face recognition, night vision and audio recording increase the impact on other people's privacy.

The [EU AI Act](/glossary/ai-act) is less relevant here than people expect. Its bans on certain uses of face recognition, in force since 2 February 2025, target things like police use of real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces and building face databases by scraping the internet or CCTV footage, according to Germany's Bundesnetzagentur. Article 2 says the Act does not set obligations for individuals using AI for purely personal, non-professional purposes. So the rules that actually bind you as a doorbell owner are the GDPR ones. Our [EU AI Act guide](/live-with-it/eu-ai-act-explained) covers the wider law.

## What about children?

Children's voices and faces get picked up like anyone else's. Google says Gemini for Home gives filtered answers to children under 18 who are set up with Voice Match, and that parental controls need a supervised Family Link account. It also suggests telling children that Gemini "is an AI tool, not a person" and not to share their address, school or family details with it. Amazon agreed to pay $25 million in a 2023 US settlement over children's privacy on Alexa, TechCrunch notes.

For face recognition, Ring itself flags that a child's consent comes with special conditions. The simplest choice is not to name children in a face library at all. Our guide to [kids, teens and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/kids-teens-and-ai-chatbots) covers the screen side of this.

## Which settings should I change?

1. **Alexa app:** go to **More**, **Alexa Privacy**, and set recordings to auto-delete after 3 months, or choose **Don't save recordings** if you can live without Voice ID. Review your voice history there too.
2. **Google Home app:** under **Home History**, set auto-delete to 3 months, and check that **Improve Google services with your voice, audio, and Gemini Live recordings** is still off.
3. **iPhone:** in Settings, check that **Improve Siri & Dictation** is off unless you chose it.
4. **Use the mute button** during private conversations, and when guests would rather not be overheard.
5. **Camera angle:** point doorbells and cameras at your own door and garden. Use privacy zones to block the street, the pavement and neighbours' property.
6. **Audio recording:** turn it off on outdoor cameras unless you really need it.
7. **Face recognition:** leave it off unless you have a clear reason. If you use it, tell visitors, don't add children, and delete profiles when asked.
8. **Encryption:** if your camera offers end-to-end encryption, consider turning it on, knowing it switches off some AI features.
9. **Shorter video storage:** pick the shortest recording history your plan allows.
10. **Shared access:** review who else in the household, or outside it, can see your camera feeds.
