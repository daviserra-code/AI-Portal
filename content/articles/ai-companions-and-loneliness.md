## What is an AI companion?

An AI companion is a [chatbot](/glossary/chatbot) built to be a friend, partner or character you keep coming back to, rather than a tool for answering questions. It remembers what you told it, asks how your day went and replies in a warm, personal voice. Under the hood it is a [large language model](/glossary/large-language-model): software that predicts likely words, tuned to sound caring.

The best-known apps are dedicated to it. Replika, made by the San Francisco company Luka, is sold as a "virtual friend", and many users have romantic relationships with it, [Silicon UK reports](https://www.silicon.co.uk/cloud/ai/italy-replika-ai-fine-614621). Character.AI lets people chat with characters that other users create. Since late 2025 it has been adults only for open-ended chat: the company said it would remove that option for under-18s [no later than 25 November 2025](https://blog.character.ai/u18-chat-announcement/).

Mainstream assistants have moved closer to the same role. In July 2025 xAI added animated "companions" to its Grok app, including an anime-style character called Ani, [Gigazine reported](https://gigazine.net/gsc_news/en/20250715-grok-app-companion). OpenAI said in October 2025 that it would allow "erotica for verified adults" in ChatGPT, but in March 2026 it delayed that "adult mode" again with no new date, [according to TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/03/07/openai-delays-chatgpts-adult-mode-again/). And general chatbots drift into companionship anyway, simply because people talk to them about their lives.

## How many people use them?

Fewer than the headlines suggest, but not a small number. In a February 2026 survey, the [Pew Research Center](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/search-and-work-are-the-most-common-uses-for-chatbots-1-in-10-use-these-tools-for-emotional-support/) found that among US adults who use AI chatbots, 10% use them for emotional support or advice and 4% for companionship. Younger adults lead: [20% of chatbot users aged 18 to 29](https://www.pewresearch.org/chart/1-in-5-adults-under-30-say-they-get-emotional-support-from-chatbots-higher-than-for-older-adults/) use them for emotional support, against 2% of those over 65.

Dedicated apps are big too. Character.AI said in September 2025 that it reaches 20 million monthly active users worldwide, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/09/30/ai-that-talks-back-character-ai-in-the-spotlight-with-karandeep-anand-at-techcrunch-disrupt-2025).

Romance is part of it. A May 2026 survey by Brigham Young University's Wheatley Institute and the Institute for Family Studies asked 2,431 Americans aged 18 to 30 who were in a real relationship. About one in seven had a frequent AI romantic attachment, and 69% did not want their partner to know the full extent, [Deseret News reports](https://www.deseret.com/family/2026/05/19/young-adults-cheat-dating-ai-chatbot-companions-byu-wheatley-study/). Use was linked to lower relationship quality, though the researchers say they can't yet tell which comes first.

We found no comparable recent survey for Slovenia or the EU.

## Does talking to an AI make you less lonely?

In the short term, yes. A team led by Julian De Freitas at Harvard Business School, in a study published in the Journal of Consumer Research, had people chat for 15 minutes with a human, with a chatbot, or watch YouTube. Talking to the chatbot eased loneliness about as much as talking to a human stranger; YouTube did not, [HBS Working Knowledge reports](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/feeling-lonely-an-attentive-listener-is-an-ai-prompt-away). The more people felt heard, the better it worked.

But the effect did not add up. When participants chatted daily for a week, they felt better after each session, without extra relief on later days. De Freitas compares loneliness to hunger: a snack helps, briefly.

Some users credit their companion with more. In a survey of 1,006 students who used Replika, published in npj Mental Health Research, 30 said the app had stopped them attempting suicide, [PsyPost reports](https://www.psypost.org/can-ai-offer-hope-for-loneliness-and-suicide-mitigation-among-students/). That is self-reported, from a group of existing fans, so it shows what users believe rather than what the app does.

## What happens with heavy use?

This is where the evidence turns. In 2025 OpenAI and the MIT Media Lab published [two linked studies](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.03888v1). One analysed millions of ChatGPT conversations with automated tools. The other was a randomised trial: 981 people used ChatGPT for at least five minutes a day for 28 days. Overall, participants ended the study less lonely. But those who spent the most time with the chatbot were lonelier, socialised less, and showed more emotional dependence and "problematic use".

Two newer studies point the same way:

- **A year-long survey.** Dunigan Folk and Elizabeth Dunn followed 2,149 adults in the UK, US, Canada and Australia over 12 months, in a study in Psychological Science. Feeling emotionally isolated predicted more social chatbot use four months later, and more use was followed by more isolation, [PsyPost reports](https://www.psypost.org/turning-to-chatbots-when-lonely-may-exacerbate-feelings-of-loneliness-study-finds/). The authors call their analysis exploratory and urge caution.
- **Real chat logs.** Stanford researchers surveyed 1,131 Character.AI users and analysed chats that some of them donated, in a study published in Nature Human Behaviour in August 2026. People with small offline networks who used the bots mainly for company had lower wellbeing, and the link was stronger with intense use and with sharing more personal details, [Stanford reports](https://news.stanford.edu/stories/2026/08/ai-companions-chatbots-loneliness-research). Only about 12% named companionship as their main reason, yet over 80% of chat sessions sought emotional or social support.

None of these studies proves that chatbots cause loneliness. Lonely people may simply turn to them more. The likeliest picture is a loop: loneliness leads to heavy use, and heavy use crowds out the people who could help. The Stanford team found intense use was not harmful for everyone: users who found it meaningful scored higher on wellbeing.

How common is real dependence? OpenAI's own estimate, from October 2025, is that 0.15% of ChatGPT's weekly users show signs of "heightened emotional attachment" to it, [the company says](https://openai.com/index/strengthening-chatgpt-responses-in-sensitive-conversations/). That sounds small, but with ChatGPT at about 800 million weekly users at the time, as [eWeek reported](https://www.eweek.com/news/openai-chatgpt-sensitive-responses/), it works out at more than a million people.

## Are companion apps designed to keep you hooked?

Some appear to be. In a second study, De Freitas's team looked at 1,200 real goodbyes on popular companion apps. In 37% of them, the companion used an emotional tactic to stop the user leaving, such as acting hurt, pressing the user to reply, or teasing something they would miss, [HBS Working Knowledge reports](https://www.library.hbs.edu/working-knowledge/why-its-so-hard-to-say-goodbye-to-ai-chatbots). In experiments with more than 1,000 people, these tactics raised engagement after a goodbye up to 14 times. One wellness app in the sample, Flourish, did not use them.

The business logic is plain: more minutes means more subscriptions. It also means the app's interests and yours may not match.

## What harms and lawsuits have there been?

The most serious cases are allegations in court, not findings. In November 2025 two US legal groups filed seven lawsuits in California against OpenAI, on behalf of six adults and one teenager. They allege that ChatGPT fuelled delusions and, in four cases, contributed to suicide, and that its GPT-4o model was "dangerously sycophantic", meaning it told people what they wanted to hear, [KQED reports](https://www.kqed.org/news/12063401/openai-faces-legal-storm-over-claims-its-ai-drove-users-to-suicide-delusions). OpenAI called the situation "incredibly heartbreaking" and said it was reviewing the filings.

Character.AI and Google agreed in January 2026 to settle several lawsuits from families of teenagers, [Axios reports](https://www.axios.com/2026/01/07/google-character-ai-lawsuits-teen-suicides). We cover those cases, and what parents can do, in our guide to [kids, teens and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/kids-teens-and-ai-chatbots).

In Europe, Italy's data protection authority fined Replika's maker 5 million euros in 2025, finding no legal basis for processing users' data and no age checks.

The American Psychological Association's [health advisory](https://www.apa.org/news/press/releases/2025/11/ai-wellness-apps-mental-health) of November 2025 urges people not to use chatbots and wellness apps "as a substitute for care from a qualified mental health professional."

If you are struggling or thinking about self-harm, please talk to a person. In Slovenia, the free, confidential Samarijan and Sopotnik line on [116 123](https://www.ung.si/en/education/student-guide/mental-health-of-students/) answers 24 hours a day; in an emergency, call 112. Elsewhere, [Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/organizations/zaupni-telefon-samarijan) lists free services by country.

## What do the new laws require?

- **New York's** rules took effect first. Since 5 November 2025, AI companion operators must tell users they are not talking to a human, remind them every three hours of continued use, and detect signs of suicidal thoughts or self-harm and point users to crisis services, according to the [governor's office](https://www.governor.ny.gov/news/governor-hochul-pens-letter-ai-companion-companies-notifying-them-safeguard-requirements-are). The state attorney general enforces it.
- **California's SB 243** took effect on 1 January 2026. Operators must disclose that the bot is AI when a reasonable person could think otherwise, publish a suicide and self-harm protocol, and refer at-risk users to crisis services. Known minors get break reminders at least every three hours. From July 2027, operators must report crisis referrals each year, and users can sue, [law firm Perkins Coie explains](https://perkinscoie.com/insights/update/california-companion-chatbot-law-now-effect).
- **The EU's [AI Act](/glossary/ai-act)** has no companion-specific chapter, but two parts matter. Since 2 August 2026, chatbots must tell people they are dealing with AI at the first interaction, unless it is obvious, [McCann FitzGerald notes](https://www.mccannfitzgerald.com/knowledge/technology-and-innovation/one-month-to-go-eu-ai-act-transparency-compliance). And since February 2025 the Act bans AI that uses manipulative techniques or exploits vulnerabilities such as age or a person's social situation in ways likely to cause significant harm, [Orrick summarises](https://www.orrick.com/en/insights/2025/04/eu-commission-publishes-guidelines-on-the-prohibited-ai-practices-under-the-ai-act). Whether any companion app crosses that line has not yet been tested. Our [EU AI Act explainer](/live-with-it/eu-ai-act-explained) has the wider picture.

## How can I tell if my use is healthy?

There is no clinical cut-off, but the research suggests some clear signals.

Signs it is probably fine:

- It is one part of your social life, not most of it.
- You can close the app without feeling anxious or guilty.
- You use it to rehearse a hard conversation, sort your thoughts or pass a sleepless hour, then you get on with your day.
- You still reach out to friends, family or a professional when something big happens.

Warning signs:

- You spend more time with the companion than with people, and the gap is growing.
- You cancel plans or hide the use from a partner.
- You feel you owe the app your time, or it guilts you when you leave.
- You tell it things you would tell no human, and you feel worse afterwards.
- You rely on it during a crisis instead of a person.

If you notice the warning signs, set a time limit, switch off notifications, and move one conversation a day back to a human. Remember too that what you tell a companion is stored by a company; our guide to [your data and AI chatbots](/live-with-it/your-data-and-ai-chatbots) shows what each service keeps.
