On 22 November 2026, the man who runs Meta's AI research posted that "superintelligence is arriving." Alexandr Wang, Meta's chief AI officer, wrote on X that AI models "are clearly going to be intelligent enough to automate all of AI research," [Fortune reports](https://fortune.com/2026/11/23/alexandr-wang-meta-superintelligence-arriving-riemann-hypothesis-nasdaq-ai-selloff/), and that it is "hard to imagine a world where humanity can maintain control" over AI that keeps improving itself without limits.

Meta is not alone. At least nine organisations now say openly that their goal is [artificial general intelligence](/glossary/agi) (AI that can do most intellectual work a person can) or [superintelligence](/glossary/superintelligence) (AI far beyond the best humans at almost everything). For what the words mean and the wider debate, start with [What is superintelligence, and who is worried about it?](/super-intelligence/what-is-superintelligence)

Everything below is a claim by the person or company named, not a measurement.

## Which companies say they are building it?

**OpenAI.** The maker of ChatGPT says its mission is "to ensure that AGI benefits all of humanity," in [principles it published in April 2026](https://openai.com/index/our-principles/). In an October 2025 livestream, chief executive Sam Altman said OpenAI was on track for an AI research assistant at the level of an intern by September 2026 and a fully automated "legitimate AI researcher" by 2028, [TechCrunch reported](https://techcrunch.com/2025/10/28/sam-altman-says-openai-will-have-a-legitimate-ai-researcher-by-2028/). Its chief scientist Jakub Pachocki said: "We believe that it is possible that deep learning systems are less than a decade away from superintelligence."

**Google DeepMind.** At the World Economic Forum in Davos in January 2026, its chief executive Demis Hassabis put genuine human-level AGI "five to 10 years" away, adding that "maybe we need one or two more breakthroughs," [according to Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/23/deepmind-demis-hassabis-anthropic-dario-amodei-yann-lecun-ai-davos/).

**Anthropic.** Anthropic makes Claude, the AI used to help draft this article. Its chief executive Dario Amodei argued in a January 2026 essay that very powerful AI could arrive within one to two years, and that "by about 2027" computers could run millions of copies of it at superhuman speed, [Fortune reports](https://fortune.com/2026/01/27/country-of-geniuses-anthropic-dario-amodei-50-million-nobel-prize-winners/). He likens it to a "country of geniuses" in a data centre and calls it "the single most serious national security threat" in a century.

**Meta.** In a July 2025 letter, Mark Zuckerberg wrote that "developing superintelligence is now in sight" and that "Meta's vision is to bring personal superintelligence to everyone," meaning an AI that helps each person "achieve your goals" ([Meta](https://www.meta.com/superintelligence/)). Wang now leads the company's Superintelligence Labs.

**xAI.** Elon Musk's company, which makes the Grok chatbot, says its mission is to "advance our collective understanding of the universe" ([xAI](https://x.ai/about/)). In February 2026 Musk merged it into his rocket company SpaceX and said that "in the long term, space-based AI is obviously the only way to scale," [Al Jazeera reported](https://aljazeera.com/news/2026/2/3/musk-merges-spacex-and-xai-firms-plans-for-space-based-ai-data-centres).

**Microsoft.** Microsoft AI, led by Mustafa Suleyman, has a "MAI Superintelligence Team" aiming for what it calls "humanist superintelligence": advanced AI kept to specific problems and designed to "keep humanity in control" ([Microsoft AI](https://microsoft.ai/blog/towards-humanist-superintelligence)).

**Safe Superintelligence Inc.** Founded by OpenAI's former chief scientist Ilya Sutskever, it calls itself "the world's first straight-shot SSI lab, with one goal and one product: a safe superintelligence" ([SSI](https://ssi.inc)).

**Alibaba.** At its September 2026 cloud conference, chief executive Eddie Wu said AI would keep "advancing toward self-iterating Artificial Superintelligence (ASI)" and that "the technical pathway" there "has become increasingly clear," [according to Alibaba Cloud](https://www.alibabacloud.com/blog/aliviews-eddie-wu-shares-alibabas-strategic-full-stack-ai-roadmap-at-the-2026-apsara-conference_603595). He gave no date.

**DeepSeek.** The Chinese lab's founder Liang Wenfeng told prospective investors in May 2026 that it would pursue "artificial general intelligence as a primary goal," [The Next Web reported](https://thenextweb.com/news/deepseek-agi-goal-10bn-funding-round), citing Bloomberg.

## How much money and computing power is behind them?

Hundreds of billions of dollars, spent mainly on chips and data centres, because today's route to more capable AI is training ever larger [models](/glossary/model) on ever more computers. Our explainer on [why AI needs so many chips](/understand/ai-chips-and-gpus) covers how that works, and [how much energy and water AI uses](/live-with-it/ai-energy-and-water) covers the cost to the grid.

- **OpenAI** raised $122 billion at a valuation of $852 billion in March 2026, [the company says](https://openai.com/index/accelerating-the-next-phase-ai/). In October 2025 Altman said it had committed to 30 gigawatts of computing infrastructure, which TechCrunch described as a $1.4 trillion obligation. Its Stargate data centre venture with Oracle and SoftBank was launched at the White House in January 2025 as a "$500 billion, 10-gigawatt commitment" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/five-new-stargate-sites/)). OpenAI has also agreed to buy an extra $250 billion of Microsoft's cloud services, [Microsoft says](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/10/28/the-next-chapter-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/).
- **Anthropic** raised $65 billion at a valuation of $965 billion in May 2026, [it announced](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h), partly to "expand compute capacity."
- **Meta** expects to spend between $135 billion and $145 billion on capital projects this year, mostly on AI infrastructure, [Fortune reports](https://fortune.com/2026/11/23/alexandr-wang-meta-superintelligence-arriving-riemann-hypothesis-nasdaq-ai-selloff/).
- **Safe Superintelligence** was valued at $32 billion in April 2025 with no product on sale, [The Decoder reported](https://the-decoder.com/openai-co-founder-sutskever-raises-2-billion-for-ai-startup-with-no-product/), citing the Financial Times.
- **Alibaba** aims for its cloud data centres worldwide to exceed 20 gigawatts of capacity by 2032.

A gigawatt is roughly the output of a large power station, so these plans are measured in power stations, not server rooms.

## What are governments doing?

Mostly backing the build-out rather than racing for superintelligence themselves.

**The United States** launched the Genesis Mission in November 2025, an effort run by the Department of Energy to use AI and federal science data to speed up research. The White House says it is "comparable in urgency and ambition to the Manhattan Project" ([White House](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2025/11/launching-the-genesis-mission/)). Its renaming of all AI as "super intelligence" is explained in SI, AI or AGI?

**China's** "AI Plus" plan, issued in August 2025, is about spreading AI through the economy rather than reaching superintelligence. It targets AI agents and smart devices being used by more than 70% of the relevant market by 2027 and more than 90% by 2030, [the Chinese government says](https://english.www.gov.cn/policies/latestreleases/202508/27/content_WS68ae7976c6d0868f4e8f51a0.html).

**The European Union** announced €20 billion in February 2025 for "AI Gigafactories", very large computing centres for training advanced models. The [European Commission](https://commission.europa.eu/topics/competitiveness/competitiveness-coordination-tool-projects/ai-gigafactories_en) estimates that building work on the first one starts in 2027.

For how these governments regulate AI, see [Who regulates AI?](/super-intelligence/who-regulates-ai)

## What have the labs promised about safety?

Each lab says it will build carefully. Most of these promises are written by the companies themselves and can be changed by them.

- **OpenAI** says it expects to work with governments and others to "ensure that we have sufficiently solved serious alignment, safety, or societal problems before proceeding further," and to "err on the side of caution in the face of uncertainty" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/our-principles/)). Its 2018 Charter said it would stop competing with, and help, a "value-aligned, safety-conscious project" that got close to AGI first. [Euronews found](https://www.euronews.com/next/2026/04/27/openai-just-changed-its-principals-heres-whats-changing) no equivalent promise in the 2026 version. OpenAI's safety and security committee sits on the board of its non-profit parent, which appoints the company's directors ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/our-structure)).
- **OpenAI and Microsoft** agreed in October 2025 that if OpenAI declares it has reached AGI, "that declaration will now be verified by an independent expert panel" ([Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2025/10/28/the-next-chapter-of-the-microsoft-openai-partnership/)).
- **Anthropic** is a public benefit corporation, meaning its directors must weigh a public mission as well as profit. An independent Long-Term Benefit Trust elects a growing share of its board, eventually a majority, [Fortune explains](https://fortune.com/2026/10/21/anthropic-adds-harvard-astrophysicist-to-the-trust-that-controls-its-board).
- **Meta** says superintelligence "will raise novel safety concerns" and that it must be "careful about what we choose to open source" ([Meta](https://www.meta.com/superintelligence/)).
- **Safe Superintelligence** says its focus keeps "safety, security, and progress" "insulated from short-term commercial pressures."

The labs' detailed testing rules, and how they have loosened, are covered in What is an AI 'frontier model'?. The research problem underneath, making an AI's goals match ours, is explained in [What do AI labs mean by 'alignment'?](/super-intelligence/what-is-ai-alignment)

The Future of Life Institute, a campaign group that wants limits on superintelligence, scored nine companies in July 2026. On "existential safety", meaning plans to keep much more capable AI under control, its reviewers said "no company exceeds C-" and called existing efforts "entirely inadequate" ([FLI](https://futureoflife.org/ai-safety-index-summer-2026/)). They also noted that several labs had weakened pledges to pause near danger points, a "moving goalpost." Anthropic scored highest overall with a C+; xAI, DeepSeek and the French company Mistral failed.

## What have they promised about sharing the benefits?

**OpenAI** became a for-profit public benefit corporation in October 2025, controlled by a non-profit, the OpenAI Foundation. The Foundation holds 26% of the company, worth about $130 billion at the time, and has promised an initial $25 billion for "health and curing diseases" and "technical solutions to AI resilience" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/built-to-benefit-everyone), [OpenAI](https://openai.com/our-structure)). Its principles promise to "resist the potential of this technology to consolidate power in the hands of the few" and aim for "universal prosperity" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/our-principles/)).

**Anthropic** describes its mission as the "responsible development and maintenance of advanced AI for the long-term benefit of humanity" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/series-h)).

**Meta** says "the benefits of superintelligence should be shared with the world as broadly as possible" ([Meta](https://www.meta.com/superintelligence/)).

**DeepSeek** reportedly plans to keep releasing [open-weights models](/glossary/open-weights-model) that anyone can download, rather than chase short-term profit, [The Next Web reports](https://thenextweb.com/news/deepseek-agi-goal-10bn-funding-round). For what "open" means in AI, see [open-source AI explained](/understand/open-source-ai).

None of these is a legal promise to share profits with the public. And as Anthropic heads towards a stock market listing, critics argue that public shareholders would have little say over its direction, [Fortune reports](https://fortune.com/2026/10/21/anthropic-adds-harvard-astrophysicist-to-the-trust-that-controls-its-board).

## What do the critics say?

The criticism comes from three directions.

**The timelines are hype.** The scientist and author Gary Marcus and colleagues argue that claims of near-AGI confuse "high performance on benchmarks (often gameable) with real world flexibility" ([Marcus](https://garymarcus.substack.com/p/rumors-of-agis-arrival-have-been)). Yann LeCun, who left Meta to start his own company, said at Davos: "We're never going to get to human-level intelligence by training LLMs or by training on text only" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/01/23/deepmind-demis-hassabis-anthropic-dario-amodei-yann-lecun-ai-davos/)). He has since raised $1.03 billion for his own lab, AMI, to try a different approach, [The Next Web reports](https://thenextweb.com/news/yann-lecun-ami-labs-world-models-billion). ([Large language models](/glossary/large-language-model) are the technology behind today's chatbots.)

**The money may not add up.** In October 2025 the Bank of England warned of a possible "sharp market correction" if AI progress or adoption disappointed, and the head of the IMF saw "worrying signs," [Tom's Hardware reported](https://www.tomshardware.com/tech-industry/bank-of-england-imf-warn-ai-bubble-risk-has-shades-of-2000-dotcom-crash-goldman-sachs-cautions-were-not-there-yet). The worry has not gone away: the weekend Wang posted, the tech-heavy Nasdaq index had wiped out its entire 2026 gain, Fortune reports.

**The safety promises are too weak.** Campaigners such as the Future of Life Institute say the labs' plans do not match the risks the labs themselves describe, and some want superintelligence banned until it is shown to be safe.

Even some builders now sound uneasy. After his post drew criticism, Wang argued that the path to superintelligence "is not inevitable" and wrote: "We should not be okay with a 'handover' of society to AI" ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2026/11/23/alexandr-wang-meta-superintelligence-arriving-riemann-hypothesis-nasdaq-ai-selloff/)). Hours before Wang's first post, OpenAI's Pachocki had proposed shared limits on AI development tied to how well the industry understands its own models, [The Next Web reports](https://thenextweb.com/news/metas-ai-chief-says-superintelligence-is-arriving-and-control-is-at-risk).

## What does this mean for you?

Nothing changes on your phone this week. But the companies making the AI you use say, in their own words, that they are trying to build something far more powerful, and they cannot yet show how they would keep it under control.

When a company says superintelligence is close, ask who is saying it, whether they are raising money at the time, and what they have promised that an outsider can check. Worth watching: whether any of these safety promises become law, whether the independent panel that is meant to verify OpenAI's AGI claim is ever convened, and whether the spending is matched by revenue.
